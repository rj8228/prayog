Prayog functional overview

What a person can do on Prayog, what the exchange does on its own, and when each thing happens. The model is an NSE-style cash equity market, based on public information only. Prayog is a simulation and is not affiliated with any exchange.

How to read this

This page describes the target product: what a production exchange in this style offers. Each feature is tagged with when Prayog builds it:

  • MVP in the current build plan (sessions S1–S22).
  • PHASE 2 next after the MVP. Needs your approval before it enters the plan.
  • LATER production features we describe so the design leaves room for them, but do not build yet.

Some things people think of as exchange features are really broker features: the broker's system holds the order and sends it to the exchange later (GTT, after-market orders, bracket orders). Those are marked as such. Prayog plays both roles, but the line matters for the design, because broker features live outside the matching engine.

The companion page, technical.html, explains how each feature is built and how data flows through the system.

1. The big picture: exchanges and trading firms

Videos about HFT describe the whole industry at once: exchanges, trading firms, feeds, FPGAs, monitoring, reporting. These belong to two different organisations on either side of a network cable:

  • The exchange (NSE, BSE, Nasdaq) runs the market. It keeps the one official order book per stock, matches orders fairly, publishes prices to everyone, and makes sure trades settle. It does not care whether you win or lose.
  • A trading firm (an HFT firm, a hedge fund, your paper-trading algo) is a customer of the exchange. It reads the exchange's price feed, decides what to trade, and sends orders. Its whole job is to make money, and in HFT, to do it faster than other firms.

Both sides have an "order book", which is a common source of confusion. The exchange's book is the real one. A trading firm rebuilds its own copy from the exchange's feed (a "book builder") so its strategy can read it without asking the exchange.

Prayog's focus in one line: build the exchange properly (the same architecture production exchanges use), add a thin broker layer for positions and P&L, and give trading firms (you, your bots, our agents) a starter kit. That kit is the SDK, which covers the trading-firm pipeline in simplified software form. The HFT-specific hardware (FPGAs, colocation, nanosecond clocks) is out of scope: it is a different, very expensive engineering field, and it doesn't change how the market itself works.

A possible later track: if you want to go deeper on the trading-firm side, the natural next steps are a backtester that replays a recorded Prayog day into a bot, firm-side risk checks in the SDK, and latency measurement from your bot to the exchange and back. It's listed in open decisions.

2. Prayog at a glance

The same picture at the level of what people do. Everything inside the exchange box happens in the moment an order arrives. The "after the trade" boxes update a moment later and can never slow down trading.

The results of "after the trade" (P&L, blotter, leaderboard) show up in the trader's terminal and through the API, read from those services rather than from the exchange.

7. Order lifecycle

Every order moves through a small set of states. Each arrow is an event the exchange publishes, so the order's state is always rebuildable from its events.

Open and Partially filled are the live states: the order can still trade, be modified or be cancelled. The other four are final. "Expired" is published as OrderCancelled with a reason; we will add an EXPIRED reason when sessions arrive in S6.

The quantities always add up: ordered = filled + open + cancelled. Prayog's property tests check this for thousands of random order flows.

8. Actions

Every action is described the same way: who does it, when it is allowed, what happens step by step, and what comes out.

A. Access

Sign in MVP

Who
Trader, ops
When
Any time
What happens
  1. The web app sends you to Keycloak's login page.
  2. Keycloak checks your password and returns a signed access token (a JWT) naming your account and roles.
  3. The web app sends that token with every request. The gateway checks the signature; it never sees your password.
Edge cases
Tokens expire after minutes and are refreshed silently. A disabled account cannot get a new token.

Create bot credentials (API key) MVP

Who
Bot developer (ops issues them in the MVP)
What happens
A Keycloak client with a client ID and secret is created. The bot trades those for a token ("client credentials" flow) and then calls the same API as the web app.
Why it matters
Bots and humans use one API, so a bot can never do something a human cannot.

Rate limiting MVP

Who
Applied by the gateway to every account
What happens
Each account has a bucket of tokens that refills at a fixed rate (for example 50 per second). Each order, modify or cancel takes a token. An empty bucket means RATE_LIMITED, and the order never reaches the engine.
Why
One runaway bot must not slow the market for everyone. Real exchanges cap message rates and charge for excessive order-to-trade ratios.

B. Orders

Place a limit order MVP

Who
Trader, bot, simulated trader
When
Session OPEN (and pre-open in Phase 2)
What happens
  1. The gateway checks the token, the rate limit and the message shape.
  2. The engine validates it: known symbol, quantity 1 to max, price on tick, price inside the band, session open, account enabled. The first failure decides the reject reason.
  3. If valid it gets an order ID and an OrderAccepted event.
  4. It matches against the opposite side while prices cross: best price first, oldest first at a price. Each match is a Trade at the resting order's price.
  5. Whatever is left rests on the book at the back of the queue for its price.
Outcomes
OrderRejected, or OrderAccepted followed by zero or more Trades
Example
Asks: 5 @ ₹100.00, 5 @ ₹100.10. You BUY 8 @ ₹100.05 → trade 5 @ ₹100.00; 3 rest as the new best bid at ₹100.05.

Place a market order MVP (S5)

When
Session OPEN
What happens
Same as a limit order, but with no price of your own: it sweeps the opposite side best price first. It stops at the price band (this protects you from buying at absurd prices in a thin book), and any remainder is cancelled with reason NO_LIQUIDITY. It never rests.
Edge cases
Empty opposite side → accepted, then fully cancelled with NO_LIQUIDITY. Many brokers turn market orders into limit orders a few percent away ("market protection"); Prayog's band limit plays that role.

Place an IOC order PHASE 2

What happens
A limit order that never rests: it fills what it can at its limit or better, and the rest is cancelled at once.
Why bots like it
No forgotten orders left on the book if the bot crashes.

Place a stop-loss order PHASE 2

What happens
  1. Accepted but kept off the visible book, waiting.
  2. When a trade prints at or through the trigger price (≤ trigger for a sell, ≥ trigger for a buy), it becomes a normal limit order and joins the back of the queue.
  3. From there it behaves as a limit order.
Edge cases
One trade can trigger many stops, and their fills can trigger more ("stop cascade"). The engine must process triggered stops in a fixed, deterministic order (trigger price, then arrival).

Modify an order MVP (S5)

Who
The order's owner
When
Session OPEN; the order is still live
What happens
  • Reduce quantity: keeps its place in the queue. You are giving up size, which harms no one queued behind you.
  • Increase quantity or change price: goes to the back of the queue at the (new) price, as if new. Otherwise you could join a queue early with 1 share and later grow to 10,000, jumping everyone.
  • New price crosses the book: it trades immediately, like a new order.
  • Reduce to the filled amount or below: treated as a cancel of the open part.
Outcomes
OrderModified (with any Trades), OrderCancelled, or OrderRejected (UNKNOWN_ORDER if already filled)

Cancel an order MVP (S5)

When
OPEN or HALTED; the order is still live
What happens
The open part is removed from the book. Shares already filled stay filled.
Race
A cancel can arrive just after the order fully filled. That is normal and gets OrderRejected(UNKNOWN_ORDER). The client must handle "too late to cancel".

Cancel all (mass cancel) PHASE 2

What happens
Cancels all of an account's live orders, optionally for one symbol. Bots use it on startup and before shutting down.

C. Exchange rules (things the exchange does by itself)

Price-time priority matching MVP

Rule
Best price first; at the same price, the earliest order first. Trades happen at the resting order's price, so an aggressive order can get a better price than its limit ("price improvement").
Why
It rewards whoever offers the best price, and then whoever offered it first. That encourages people to show liquidity early.

Price band rejection MVP (S6)

Rule
A limit price outside today's band is rejected with PRICE_OUTSIDE_BAND. Market orders stop at the band edge.
Why
It limits damage from mistakes and panic: no single day can move a price more than the band.

Self-trade prevention MVP (S6)

Rule
If an incoming order would trade with a resting order from the same account, the incoming order's remainder is cancelled with SELF_TRADE_PREVENTION. Trades already made with other accounts stand.
Why
Trading with yourself creates fake volume (a "wash trade"), which regulators forbid. Other modes exist (cancel the resting order, or both); cancel incoming is simplest and the most common default.

Expiry at end of day MVP (S6)

When
When the session moves to CLOSED
What happens
Every live DAY order is cancelled, in a fixed order (by symbol, then order ID), so a replay produces the same events.

Opening call auction PHASE 2

What happens
  1. During order entry, orders collect without matching.
  2. At the end, the exchange tries every candidate price and picks the one where the most shares can trade. Ties go to the smallest leftover imbalance, then the price closest to the previous close.
  3. All crossing orders trade at that one price. The leftovers move into continuous trading.
Why
Overnight news piles up. One fair opening price is better than a chaotic first second of trading.

Circuit breakers LATER

What happens
Stock level: the price band already acts as a circuit filter. Market level: when the index falls 10%, 15% or 20% the whole market halts for a set time (SEBI's index circuit breakers). In Prayog this would be an automatic HALTED state.

D. Market data

Order book (depth) MVP (S11)

What you get
On subscribe, a snapshot of every price level (price, total shares, number of orders). After that, an update for each level that changes. Each message has a sequence number, so a gap tells you to ask for a fresh snapshot.
Levels
Aggregated by price ("level 2"). Individual orders are not shown, and neither are account IDs.

Trade prints and ticker MVP (S11)

What you get
Every trade, anonymous: price, quantity, time, and which side was the aggressor. The terminal builds the last price, day high/low, volume and candlestick charts from these.

Your own order updates MVP (S11)

What you get
A private stream of your own orders' events (accepted, filled, cancelled, rejected). Only you see it.

E. After the trade

Positions MVP (S16)

What
Shares held per account and symbol: buys add, sells subtract. A negative position is a short sale.
When
Updated from each trade event a moment after the trade (asynchronously, off the order path).

P&L MVP (S16)

Realised
Profit locked in by closing a position. Computed against your average buy price (or FIFO lots; see decisions).
Unrealised
Profit on paper for what you still hold, valued at the last traded price. It moves with every trade print.
Net
Realised + unrealised − charges

Charges MVP (simple) · LATER (Indian detail)

MVP
A flat brokerage per trade plus a percentage fee, set in config.
Later
The full Indian stack: brokerage, STT, exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fee, stamp duty (buy side) and GST on brokerage and fees. Each has its own base and rate, and rounding rules matter.

Trade blotter and reports MVP (S19)

What
A list of your orders and trades with times, prices and charges, plus a P&L summary. The production version adds a daily contract note (the legal trade confirmation) and CSV export.

Leaderboard MVP (S17)

What
Accounts ranked by net P&L, updated live. Shows totals only, never individual trades.

Settlement LATER

What
In India, shares and cash actually change hands one business day after the trade (T+1). Until then the shares are "unsettled". Prayog would simulate cash and holdings balances and a daily settlement run.

Buying power and margin checks PHASE 2

What
Before accepting an order, check that the account can pay for it (cash minus what open orders have already reserved) or has enough shares to sell. In the MVP, accounts have unlimited money: fine for a game, wrong for a realistic broker.
Design impact
This check must happen on the order path and be deterministic, so balances live inside the engine (or a risk stage before it), not in the database.

F. Operations

Open, halt, resume, close the market MVP

Who
Ops, or the sim clock at the configured times
What happens
A SessionStateChanged event. Halting keeps the book but stops matching; closing expires DAY orders.

Kill switch MVP

Global
Ops sets the market to HALTED. Everything stops at once.
Per account
Ops disables one account: all its live orders are cancelled (reason KILL_SWITCH) and new ones are rejected (ACCOUNT_DISABLED). This is the tool for a misbehaving bot.

Change clock speed MVP

What happens
Ops changes the multiplier (1×, 10×, 60×). Sim time runs faster; nothing else changes. Because ticks are recorded, a replay is still exact.

Replay a session MVP (S8)

What happens
Feed the recorded input journal back into a fresh engine. The output must be byte-for-byte identical (checked with a SHA-256 checksum). This is how bugs are reproduced and how the exchange recovers after a crash.

Add or suspend an instrument PHASE 2

What happens
In the MVP, instruments come from config at startup. Later they become commands that go through the journal, so they replay too.

G. Simulated market

Simulated traders MVP (S13)

Kinds
  • Market makers keep a buy and a sell order around a fair price and earn the spread. They are why the book is never empty.
  • Momentum traders buy after rises and sell after falls.
  • Mean-reversion traders do the opposite.
  • Noise traders trade randomly in small size and keep volume flowing.
  • News shocks move each stock's hidden "fair value" now and then, so prices trend.
Rule
They use the public API like any bot: no special access. Their random choices are seeded, but because orders travel over the network their arrival order varies, so a run is not exactly repeatable. The exchange's replay is.

9. Worked example: one trader's morning

Instrument ABC: tick 5 paise, reference ₹100.00, band 10% (₹90.00–₹110.00). Charges for the example: ₹10 per trade.

Sim timeActionBook before (best)ResultPosition · avg price
09:15:00Ops opens the marketemptySessionStateChanged(OPEN)—
09:15:02Market maker quotes 100 @ ₹99.95 / 100 @ ₹100.05—Two orders rest—
09:20:10You BUY 10 @ ₹100.10 (limit)ask ₹100.05Trade 10 @ ₹100.05 (resting price: 5 paise saved)+10 · ₹100.05
09:31:00You BUY 10 @ ₹101.95ask ₹102.05No cross; your bid rests and becomes the best bid+10
09:33:40Momentum bot SELLS 10 at marketbid ₹101.95 (yours)Trade 10 @ ₹101.95; you are filled+20 · ₹101.00
10:05:00You SELL 15 @ ₹103.00bid ₹103.10Trade 15 @ ₹103.10+5 · ₹101.00
10:30:00Last trade on ABC prints at ₹102.50—Unrealised P&L is revalued+5

Average price after both buys: (10 × 100.05 + 10 × 101.95) ÷ 20 = ₹101.00.
Realised on the sale: (103.10 − 101.00) × 15 = ₹31.50.
Unrealised on the 5 left: (102.50 − 101.00) × 5 = ₹7.50.
Charges: 3 trades × ₹10 = ₹30.00. Net P&L: 31.50 + 7.50 − 30.00 = ₹9.00.

Internally every one of those numbers is whole paise (₹101.00 = 10,100 paise). An average price that isn't a whole number of paise is kept as a total cost (paise × shares) instead, so no rounding happens until display.
